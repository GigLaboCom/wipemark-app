## Deviations from the document, and why

- **One case table instead of one function body per input.** Every fixed
  input of §5.3 is a row of `CASES` (`test`, `input`, `expect`); each
  named test runs the rows filed under it and asserts the **whole**
  `brief` vector for each, as §5.1 asks. The reason is the two
  corpus-wide tests: `a_kept_hit_is_a_likely_false_positive_…` and
  `what_is_kept_stays_kept_on_the_output` must walk "every input of this
  table", and a table they share cannot drift from the named tests the
  way a copied list can. The two 32/33-code-point flags are generated
  (`long_flag`) and asserted in their test's body.
- **Corpus size 64, not 59.** §5.3 says "59 texts × 4 combinations". The
  corpus here is every input of §5.3 (47 fixed rows + the 2 generated
  flags), the 12 keep fixtures and the 3 D34/D35 texts = 64; all pass
  under all four combinations. The document's 59 presumably counted a
  slightly different set of inputs; no input was dropped.
- **Two mutation descriptions of §5.3 are not what the mutation
  produces; both mutations still paint the test red.**
  (1) `what_is_kept_stays_kept_on_the_output` (2), D34 on `next`: the
  document says `[1780 200C 17B4]` keeps `[200C 17B4]`, then `[17B4]`.
  Under the mutation the ZWNJ is kept, and a kept ZWNJ is not glue, so
  it becomes `prev_kept` (§4.2.4 step 4) and U+17B4 then has no Khmer
  anchor: kept `[200C]`, then `[]` on the output. Red either way; the
  report records what happened.
  (2) `script_format_controls_are_never_findings` (1) "→ (a), (b), (c)
  red": part (a) panics first at U+0600, so (b) and (c) are not reached
  in the same run.
- **`every_keep_fixture_survives_whole` also compares the bytes read by
  `include_str!` with those read by `include_bytes!`**, and checks that
  `keep-leading-bom.txt` starts with U+FEFF — a fixture whose BOM an
  editor dropped would otherwise still have a plausible hit count of 0.
- No change to the module map or to any interface of README §3.2–3.3,
  and CLAUDE.md was not touched. Nothing in CLAUDE.md disagrees with the
  document.

## What the next documents should know

- **E1-3.** `hits` is the whole context pass: one `Hit` per
  finding-capable code point except a U+FEFF at byte 0, `at` strictly
  increasing, `class == class_of(c)`, never `Homoglyph`, `replacement`
  `None`; `kept_by_context` ⇒ `LikelyFalsePositive`, otherwise exactly
  `class.max_confidence()`. A kept hit must be kept whatever `Options`
  say (A §5.2, Q-A1). The idempotence the scrubber needs holds for the
  context pass under "remove every not-kept hit; an exotic space stays or
  becomes U+0020; a soft hyphen may stay" — that is the `apply` helper in
  `context.rs`'s tests, which mirrors E1-3's decision and is not its
  API. A leading BOM produces no hit and must be **kept** in the output
  (D33). Delete the two `dead_code` allowances on `Hit`/`hits` when
  `collect_hits` calls it. `TextStats::of` is the one constructor; D28's
  "over the source" is a single call.
- **E1-3 fixtures.** `fixtures/text/` exists now with the twelve
  `keep-*` files; `clean_is_idempotent_on_every_fixture` should walk
  them too. The standardized-variant pairs U+2229 U+FE00 and U+0030
  U+FE00 are in `keep-standardized-variants.txt`, as §5.5 said.
- **E1-4.** No letter of Latin, Cyrillic or Greek is finding-capable
  (the walk test's table shows the only finding-capable letters are the
  four Hangul fillers), so `homoglyph::hits` and `context::hits` never
  share an offset; merging the two streams by `at` needs no tie-break.
- **E1-5.** `letter_shares` is in percent (0–100) and is 0.0 for every
  share when `letters == 0`; `ScriptGuard`'s `min_letters` should read
  `LetterShares::letters`. Marks are never letters; U+30FC and the
  mathematical alphanumerics are *other*. Delete the `dead_code`
  allowances on `LetterShares`/`letter_shares` when the guard calls it.
- **Every later document.** `class_of` costs a short series of binary
  searches; `context::hits` calls it a handful of times per code point
  (each rule re-checks D34 on its neighbours). The full walk over 1.1 M
  scalars takes about two seconds in a debug build, so a megabyte of text
  is well under that.
