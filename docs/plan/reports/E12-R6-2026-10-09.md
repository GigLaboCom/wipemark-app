### How it works, in one paragraph

`route` takes the planar path only for a lossy source whose planes are
`Some`, 4:2:0 or 4:2:2, of the raster's size, 8-bit RGB. The proof
(`verify_with`) keeps `k*` and `E(1)/E(0)` on luma and replaces the
out-of-range share: per pixel of the support (`NOISE_FLOOR ≤ α < opaque`),
out when its stored Y lies outside `[α·L_Y, α·L_Y + (1−α)·255]` by more than
8, or its chroma block's stored Cb or Cr outside `[ᾱ·L_C, ᾱ·L_C + (1−ᾱ)·255]`
by more than `blend_levels_c` (9 at q95, 9.5 at q90, 10.5 at q85); the share
is the pixels with either. The inverse divides Y by `α` and each block's
chroma by `ᾱ` (the block's mean over its pixels inside the picture, `α` 0
outside the template), unrounded; upsamples the restored chroma with the
decoder's triangle filter in reals (the same near/far rule as
`Planes::to_rgb`); recombines with the decoder's 14-bit constants read as
reals; rounds half away from zero and clamps once; and writes only pixels
inside the rectangle that are no hole (`α` or `ᾱ` ≥ `opaque_above`) and whose
`α` or block `ᾱ` is at the noise floor or over. Then D246's noise, the
outline, step, colour step and texture exactly as the RGB restoration. The
second pass and `prove`'s re-examination of the 4:4:4 output are RGB. Several
first-pass marks are each inverted from the stored planes.

### Where the planar consistency goes when R6 and R7 merge

* **Where**: in `planar::restore` (`crates/wipemark-pixels/src/planar.rs`),
  after the write loop and before D246's noise, from the `inverse` it
  already holds: `let back = inverse.blend_back(raster, chroma);` and R7's
  measure function over `back.pairs`, `back.excluded` as
  `consistency_excluded`, into the `Restored { … }` literal at the end of
  that function — which will not compile after the merge until the fields
  are there, so it cannot be forgotten. `consistency_dct` is `None` here.
* **Which samples** (`Inverse::blend_back`, R7 §4):
  * **Y**, per pixel the restoration wrote with `NOISE_FLOOR ≤ α < opaque`:
    `α·L_Y + (1 − α)·Y(restored RGB)` against the stored `Y_I`, full
    resolution;
  * **chroma**, per block with `NOISE_FLOOR ≤ ᾱ < opaque`, at the chroma's
    own resolution: `ᾱ·L_C + (1 − ᾱ)·C_rec(q)` against the stored
    `C_I,sub`, Cb and Cr, where `C_rec(q)` is the restored RGB's chroma
    brought back to the block by the **left inverse of the decoder's
    upsampler** — per axis `(3·(u₂ₖ + u₂ₖ₊₁) − (u₂ₖ₋₁ + u₂ₖ₊₂))/4` over
    the four pixels around the block (exact on a triangle-filtered plane);
  * **left out**, counted in samples: a pixel with a sample clamped (its
    unrounded RGB more than half a level outside 0–255), a block whose
    4 × 4 support holds one or a hole or leaves the picture;
  * the p95 over **both** sets, in stored levels (`back.luma` says where Y
    ends, if a split is wanted).
* **Why not the encoder's box average for chroma**: the triangle filter
  followed by a 2 × 2 box is a `[1, 6, 1]/8` smoothing, not the identity,
  and measures the upsampler. Measured on the five 4:2:0 fixtures: chroma
  p95 1.56–1.81 by the box (overall p95 1.00–1.12, over the 1-level bound on
  three of five), 0.81–1.01 by the left inverse (overall p95 0.24–0.30,
  Y alone 0.18–0.19). Q3.
* **The test**: replace `consistency_px(&r)` in
  `crates/wipemark-picture/tests/planar.rs` with `r.consistency_px`, remove
  the stub and the `#[ignore]`. Its mutation, "drop the chroma
  upsampling's last row", is in `Inverse::chroma_up` (the restored rows); by
  the figures above, overall p95 ≤ 1.0 holds on every 4:2:0 fixture, and
  chroma p95 alone reaches 1.01 on `victory-1025-q98-420`.

### The RGB cube in the planar model (Q1's figure)

The planar inverse's colour per pixel — `Y_O(p)`, `Cb_O,sub`, `Cr_O,sub` of
its block — brought to RGB with the exact JFIF inverse, the excess outside
0–255 scaled by `1 − max(α, ᾱ)` into stored levels, the share over 8
(`planar_measure`, `blend_back.cube`; a prototype in the tool, not in the
library). Last column of the variants' table: **q95 0.00–0.06 %**, **q90
4:2:0 1.13–1.95 %**, **q90 4:2:2 0.55–1.45 %**, **q85 1.69–3.81 %** —
about the RGB path's own shares (1.03–2.17 % where it refused). So D252's
q90 refusals on the stickers are **not** mostly the chroma model: the
codec's error, amplified, takes the restored colour out of the cube in R
(the green's R is 3–12) by as much in the right model as in the wrong one.
What lifts them under D495 as written is that a per-plane interval cannot
see that: Y ≈ 97, Cb ≈ 104, Cr ≈ 65 are each far from 0 and 255.

## Deviations from the document

1. **The fixture tests are in `crates/wipemark-picture/tests/planar.rs`**,
   not `crates/wipemark-pixels/tests/planar.rs`: the pixels crate has no
   codec, and the picture crate decodes the planes by the road a user's
   file takes (`decode_with_planes`). The synthetic tests are in the
   pixels file.
2. **The synthetic subsampled JPEGs** come from a new
   `wipemark_pixels::synth::jpeg_planes` (`#[doc(hidden)]`): IJG's tables at
   the quality, JFIF's matrix, block-averaged chroma, an 8 × 8 DCT, the
   quantiser and back, in reals — not libjpeg's integer arithmetic, so a
   stored sample can be a level from Pillow's. No encoder in the tree
   subsamples (`image` writes 4:4:4 only).
3. **"A 0.7-opacity white sparkle"** is read as the profile's own shape at
   0.7 of its opacity, over a yellow whose blue is at the floor, against a
   catalogue of V1 alone: with V2 in the synthetic catalogue, 0.7 of V1 is
   V2's own opacity (0.72) and is a true mark. A sparkle at a *higher*
   opacity than the profile's can never be out of range under a white logo
   (in RGB or in the planes), so it could not hold the share over 1 %.
4. **The consistency pairs' chroma** is read back through the left inverse
   of the decoder's upsampler, not the encoder's box average (figures
   above; Q3).
5. **The mask's mutation** is "every non-hole pixel of the rectangle
   written from the planar RGB", not "the whole ROI from `to_rgb`": the
   restoration never calls `to_rgb` (it upsamples in reals, before the one
   rounding). It tests the same mask.
6. **`Planar::Unavailable`** is set by `clean_bytes_with_planes` on a
   restoration of a lossy three-component JPEG whose planes were not read;
   a refusal has no field for it, and `examine_with` cannot tell a JPEG
   from a lossy WebP, so it is not set there.
7. **Holes**: a pixel of a block whose `ᾱ` ≥ `opaque_above` counts as a hole
   whatever its own `α` (§4.2 "every pixel of the block a hole").
8. **The share's unit** is pixels on the planar path (samples on the RGB
   one), as §4.3's "a pixel is out when…" reads; the 1 % bound is the same
   number.
9. **Added beyond the document**: `examine_planar_at`, `planar::invert` /
   `Inverse` / `BlendBack` (doc-hidden, for R7 and the tool),
   `inspect_bytes_with_planes`, `blend_levels_c` / `DC_SHARE`, the five
   added tests and one measurement above, `planar_measure`, three scripts;
   `verify::verify` is kept as a `#[cfg(test)]` wrapper of `verify_with`
   for its in-crate test. Two `real.rs` tests are ignored under the feature
   only.
10. `apps/wipemark-cli/src/image.rs` (a test's `Restored` literal) is
    touched, outside the document's file list.
