## Для владельца

Проверили, помогает ли нейросеть убирать знак Grok так, как это делает
GeminiWatermarkTool. Его способ: сначала обратная формула по карте знака, потом
нейросеть FDnCNN, которая сглаживает то, что осталось. У него внутри ровно та же
открытая модель FDnCNN (веса KAIR), только записанная с половинной точностью.

**Что показали пять настоящих картинок.** На ровном сером фоне знак уходит
начисто любым способом, в том числе нашей программой. На остальных четырёх одна
обратная формула оставляет контур, потому что карта знака пока снята с одной
картинки. Нейросеть в режиме GeminiWatermarkTool этот контур примерно вдвое
приглушает, но на месте знака остаётся гладкое «мыльное» пятно: на
штукатурке, ткани и земле фактура пропадает. Нейросеть по нашим правилам почти
ничего не меняет.

**Что показала проверка, где известно, что было под знаком** (60 фонов, светлый и
тёмный варианты, четыре размера, сжатие как у X и слабее). После нашей программы
наша нейросеть прибавляет 0,05–0,1 дБ, это ничего. Способ GeminiWatermarkTool с
мягкой настройкой (σ 15) прибавляет 2,7–6,1 дБ, и это много. Но фактура под
знаком у него в норме только на 7–10 % вырезок против 24–29 % у одной программы,
и картинка уходит от того, что было записано в файле. Это выигрыш сглаживанием,
а не восстановлением. На проверочных фонах сглаживание выигрывает, потому что
фоны там ровные или с текстом, фотографий в наборе пока нет. С настройкой по
умолчанию (σ 50) GeminiWatermarkTool местами хуже одной программы.

**Вывод по нашему правилу отбора: нейросеть для Grok сейчас не нужна.** Брать
способ GeminiWatermarkTool в программу не советую: он прячет след, размазывая
фактуру, и это видно глазом на настоящих картинках. Главное, чего не хватает для
Grok, — точная карта знака на каждый размер (по чёрным и белым картинкам из
списка первого разбора) и поиск тёмного знака (D540). После этого проверку стоит
повторить.

## N1 — GWT's denoiser, as it is

GWT cloned at `7c6a99f2c3df97eb3c430ef87a0c962aea5cb80e` — its `main` on
2026-10-11 **and** the commit `crates/wipemark-pixels/marks/gwt/` came from (one
commit, so the two readings the task asked for are one).

| question | GWT's answer | where |
|---|---|---|
| the network | FDnCNN colour: 20 convolutions 3 × 3, 64 channels, ReLU between (19 `convrelu` + 1 `conv`), input `[R, G, B, σ/255]`, output the clean picture (not a residual) | `external/ncnn/model-convert/output/model_core.id.h`; `src/core/ai_denoise.cpp:209–249` |
| the weights | embedded as ncnn FP16 (`model_core.mem.h`, 1 340 124 bytes of `.bin`), FP16 storage, FP32 arithmetic | `src/core/ai_denoise_model.cpp:1–42`; `ai_denoise.cpp:348–352` |
| are they KAIR's? | **yes**: every one of the 667 584 weights is KAIR's `fdncnn_color.pth` rounded to FP16 — relative difference ≤ 9.7·10⁻⁴ where \|w\| > 10⁻³, correlation 0.999999976; the 2 190 sign flips are values under 6.1·10⁻⁵ in magnitude, FP16's subnormals; the 1 219 biases (FP32) equal to the bit | `gwt-way.py weights` → `weights.json` |
| how `--sigma` reaches the network | a **scalar**: a uniform fourth plane `σ/255`, σ clamped to 0–150 | `ai_denoise.cpp:155–178, 224–226, 404` |
| masked by α? | **no**, by the map's **edges**: Sobel magnitude of α → normalised → √ → dilated (5 × 5 ellipse) → Gaussian σ 2 → × strength → cut at 1; embedded in the ROI padded by 32 (clipped to the picture), Gaussian σ 1 over it; `out = w·den + (1 − w)·in`. Pixels outside the mark (inside the padded ROI) can move | `ai_denoise.cpp:270–311, 408–489` |
| the order | detect → reverse alpha blend (`α < 0.002` untouched, α capped at 0.99, clamp to [0, 255], 8 bits) → denoise → save | `src/cli/cli_app.cpp:520–541`; `src/core/blend_modes.cpp:33–108` |
| defaults | `--denoise ai`: σ **50**, strength **1.2**, padding 32 (CLI and GUI alike); the CLI's denoise is off unless asked; the README suggests `--sigma 15` "for anime / illustration content" (its video tool's section) | `cli_app.cpp:190–222, 918–928`; `src/gui/app/app_state.hpp:211–225`; `README.md:143` |
| one quirk | the CLI and GUI hand the denoiser the **large** map whatever size was removed (resized to the region) | `cli_app.cpp:536`; `app_controller.cpp:237–238` |

So one weights file serves both: GWT's rule is KAIR's network at FP16, and
`gwt-way.py` runs it in FP32 through onnxruntime — the FP16 rounding is below a
level at the output. `fdncnn_export.py`'s own check: the ONNX model against
torch, max |Δ| 2.5·10⁻⁶ on a 4 × 64 × 80 input; 668 803 parameters.

**The two GWT rules measured** (N2, N3): `gwt-ai` = σ 50, strength 1.2 (its
default) and `gwt-ai-s15` = σ 15, strength 1.2 (the README's lighter setting).

**What each column does** (there is no truth on a real picture; nothing here is
called restored that the product did not prove):

* **GWT-inverse.** Exactly the product's formula without its proof. On the grey
  picture — the map's own — it leaves nothing (step +0.1, consistency 0.3). On
  the other four the map is not the mark Grok drew there (another scale, another
  variant, a resampled edge — the first look's finding), and the inverse leaves a
  band 7–20 levels off its surroundings: an outline you see at once on the blue
  and the brown, fainter on the fabric and the plaster.
* **+ gwt-ai (σ 50).** Halves the band where it was large (−19.6 → −11.3,
  −14.4 → −6.7, +7.3 → +0.5) by **smoothing the whole wordmark's box**: the
  texture under it falls to 0.28–0.41 of its surroundings, and the result leaves
  what the file says by 13–28 levels (`consistency_px`). On the plaster and the
  brown it is a visible smooth patch. Its pixels are *reconstructed* (D499).
* **+ gwt-ai-s15.** Less smoothing (texture 0.32–0.97), the band about as large
  as with σ 50 on the light pictures, smaller than the inverse's on the dark ones.
* **+ R10 (k5-s1.0-rgb).** Its σ map starts at the picture's `σ_base` (0.3–6

  the band, and changes nothing on the grey one. It cannot hide an outline the
  map's mismatch left, and is not meant to.
* **product.** Only the grey picture is proved (inverted, as the first look did:
  the product does not propose a dark mark, D540 proposed); its result is as
  clean as GWT-inverse's (step −0.0) with the texture nearer its surroundings
  (0.81 against 0.56). The other four are refused by the product (edges or
  range) and get no column: a file written with the mark left is a re-encode,
  not a restoration.

bench cannot propose the dark one either (the product's signed NCC).

## Deviations, and what went wrong

* **The pictures are in git.** The task said no picture enters git; the owner
  said during the run (2026-10-11) that pictures may be committed as examples
  where needed. The five originals are `fixtures/image/grok-x/` byte for byte,
  with their sha256 in `fixtures/image/README.md` and a `binary` line in
  `.gitattributes`. Crops, maps, the catalogue and the weights stay out.
* **`evalkit.Crop.logo`** read a profile's logo from the shipped catalogue only,
  so every crop of a `--catalogue` profile was refused. It now falls back to the
  logo the bench drew the crop with (`meta.blend.logo`), with a selftest
  (`a_catalogue_files_profile_takes_the_logo_it_was_drawn_with`, red without the
  fallback).
* **No separate X slice**: X's tables are libjpeg's q95 (above).
* **The dark variant through the negatives**, not through `grok-x-dark` (above).
* **F2 on run B**: the negatives' run has one group (`inv`); `verdict
  --inv-tiles` gives each record its source's group back (the tiles in name
  order), so F2 reads text on both runs. `fdncnn_run.py`'s own report on run B
  shows F2 as "–" for that reason.
* **R10's "best" variant** before N3 was `k5-s1.0-rgb` (N2's column); N3's best
  by median gain is `k10-s1.5-rgb` (N4's).
* **A slip, undone**: the first attempt at the venv failed (`/usr/local/bin/python3`
  is a link whose `venv` cannot find its standard library) and the `pip install`
  that followed ran against the system Python, putting `onnx`, `onnxruntime`,
  `protobuf`, `flatbuffers` and `ml_dtypes` into `/opt/python3.13`. They were
  uninstalled at once (and the empty `google/` folder they left removed); the
  venv was then made from `/opt/python3.13/bin/python3.13` and everything runs
  from it.
