## What was built

* `decode` remembers a JPEG's component count (`Source::Jpeg { components }`).
* `encode_like` writes a JPEG with `image::codecs::jpeg::JpegEncoder` at
  `JPEG_QUALITY = 95` — grey (`L8`) when the original had one component and
  every pixel is still grey, RGB otherwise; `Encoding::Jpeg { quality }`.
* `restorable`: a lossy WebP (lossless out, `from_lossy`), a 1- or
  3-component JPEG; a CMYK JPEG is examined, reported, not restored.
* The proof for a lossy output: `psnr(decoded, restored) ≥ PSNR_FLOOR`
  (34 dB), beside "nothing outside moved" and "nothing verifies".
  `psnr` is public.
* `image` (feature `jpeg`) moved from a dev- to a normal dependency of
  `wipemark-picture`; the lock did not move.
* `tests/lossy.rs` (4 tests); E12-3's `a_jpeg_is_examined_and_not_yet_restored`
  is gone — what it asserted is no longer true, and `a_marked_jpeg_is_restored_and_re_encoded`
  asserts the opposite.

## Decisions (I-numbers; mapped from D188 by the series report)

| I | decision |
|---|---|
| I28 | **4:4:4 out** — `image`'s `JpegEncoder` has no subsampling setting (its documentation says 4:2:2; its code builds every component at 1×1). "The input's subsampling where the encoder allows" is therefore never; 4:4:4 loses less than any input's |
| I29 | **A grey JPEG stays grey** when every restored pixel is grey; otherwise RGB |
| I30 | **A CMYK JPEG is not restored** — its ICC profile, kept by `reframe`, describes inks the YCbCr re-encode would not have; the finding is reported and is a mark left |
| I31 | **The lossy proof is a PSNR floor of 34 dB** against the restored raster — far below what quality 95 gives a picture it just encoded (the suite prints the measured value), far above a wrong picture |

D158 is amended as the owner answered: no coefficient codec, no block
patch; a full re-encode at quality 95, said by `Encoding::Jpeg`. D159
holds: C2PA leaves whenever pixels changed.
